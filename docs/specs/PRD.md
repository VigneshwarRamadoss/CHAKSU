# CHAKSU Commerce Product Requirements Document

**Status:** Production blueprint v1.0  
**Product:** CHAKSU B2C premium streetwear storefront  
**Market:** India first; international-ready  
**Primary audience:** Culture-aware 18-32-year-olds shopping on mobile  
**North-star:** Full-price customers who return, not traffic that merely looks impressive

## 1. Executive decision

CHAKSU will be a premium Indian streetwear commerce experience built around **controlled aggression**: decisive typography, cinematic South-Indian night imagery, edited product choice, visible construction proof, and unusually clear service.

The site must create desire in the first viewport and remove doubt before checkout. It must not become a portfolio that happens to sell clothing, a catalogue with cinematic decoration, or a smaller imitation of Almost Gods, Bluorng, Bonkers Corner, Urban Monkey, or VYBN.

### Positioning hypothesis

> CHAKSU is for culture-aware young Indians who want expressive streetwear that feels designed, moves comfortably, and is easy to trust. It differentiates through an ownable typographic identity, climate-aware product proof, tightly edited releases, and service clarity.

This is a launch hypothesis. Validate it with 12-15 target-customer interviews, five-second comprehension tests, and a pre-order or waitlist experiment before committing the full first-drop inventory.

### One-line product promise

**Statement silhouettes. Proof in every detail.**

### Brand behavior

- **Controlled:** Few strong choices, strict art direction, clear interface hierarchy.
- **Aggressive:** Cropped display type, decisive scale, abrupt editorial transitions.
- **Credible:** Fit, fabric, make, delivery, and returns are visible where decisions happen.
- **Participatory:** Customers and creators complete a drop after launch.
- **Indian without costume:** Place is expressed through people, climate, movement, material, and language rather than borrowed symbols.

## 2. Evidence translated into product decisions

| Evidence from supplied research | Product decision for CHAKSU |
| --- | --- |
| Bonkers wins with breadth and newness but creates catalogue overload and policy friction. | Launch with an edited range, intent-led discovery, and no permanent sale theatre. |
| Bluorng creates price power through a coherent brand world, product proof, and moment-based drops, but its policy contradictions tax trust. | Build a coherent world, but make service clarity a premium signal on every PDP. |
| Urban Monkey owns a memorable entry category and a culture-to-commerce loop, but category sprawl and discounting weaken focus. | Name one hero franchise and one collectible entry item; expand only after repeat behavior is proven. |
| The VYBN strategy identifies an implicit proposition, weak product proof, and a story-shop disconnect. | Put one promise above the fold and connect every editorial story directly to products and proof. |
| The Almost Gods reference video uses cinematic entry, mega-menu editorial imagery, sparse grids, asymmetric content, editorial interludes, and a sticky PDP rail. | Adopt the interaction grammar, not the visual identity: CHAKSU uses its own typography, palette, Chennai-after-dark imagery, proof modules, and accessible type scale. |
| The supplied typography direction identifies the CHAKSU letterform itself as a memory asset, especially the central K. | Build the interface around a clean master wordmark and a repeatable K-derived motion/crop device. |

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### Business goals

1. Establish a recognisable CHAKSU memory system before expanding assortment.
2. Convert first-time mobile visitors at full price by reducing fit, quality, delivery, and return uncertainty.
3. Increase average order value through coordinated fits and useful cross-sell rather than discount ladders.
4. Turn each drop into a repeatable content and owned-audience event.
5. Build first-party customer insight: fit objections, climate needs, return reasons, and product-role performance.

### Customer goals

1. Understand CHAKSU and the current release in less than five seconds.
2. Find an outfit or product without processing a large catalogue.
3. See how the garment fits, moves, feels, and is made.
4. Know the delivered price, delivery window, payment options, and return path before purchase.
5. Buy quickly on mobile, then track, exchange, review, and re-enter the brand.

### Non-goals for launch

- Marketplace functionality or multi-vendor inventory.
- Hundreds of SKUs or deep category breadth.
- Native mobile apps.
- A points-heavy loyalty economy.
- AR try-on, virtual worlds, or speculative Web3 mechanics.
- Continuous discounting, fake countdowns, or false scarcity.
- Fully bespoke checkout when a compliant hosted checkout is safer.
- Heavy scroll effects on every section.

## 4. Target customers and jobs

### Persona A: The scene builder

- **Age/context:** 20-29; creator, dancer, musician, designer, photographer, skater, or adjacent participant.
- **Need:** Recognition by the right people without wearing a costume.
- **Trigger:** A distinctive coordinated silhouette attached to real cultural authorship.
- **Objection:** “Is this authentic, or is it borrowing a scene?”
- **Website response:** Creator context, documented process, city-specific stories, and a product that stands without the story.

### Persona B: The style explorer

- **Age/context:** 18-24; college or first job; discovers through Reels and friends.
- **Need:** An outfit transformation with manageable purchase risk.
- **Trigger:** Clear styling, fit comparison, entry product, COD/UPI, and peer proof.
- **Objection:** “Will the oversized fit work on me, and is it worth the price?”
- **Website response:** Visual fit guide, model measurements, garment measurements, one-click complete-the-fit, and transparent exchange terms.

### Persona C: The quiet signaler

- **Age/context:** 25-34; creative, tech, founder, or design-aware professional.
- **Need:** Premium daily uniforms with detail and restraint.
- **Trigger:** Material proof, useful construction, coordinated pieces, and fast service.
- **Objection:** “Is this too loud, too hot, or too fragile for regular wear?”
- **Website response:** Climate/wear guidance, close-up construction, movement video, care proof, and clear delivery.

### Jobs to be done

| Job | Customer question | Required product response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Belong | Will the right people recognise this? | Ownable silhouette, story, creator/customer proof. |
| Express | Does this feel like me? | Styling variety, colorways, detail, strong art direction. |
| Move | Will this work in real Indian life? | Breathability context, movement video, pockets, care, climate tags. |
| Trust | Will the fit, quality, delivery, and service hold? | Standardized proof stack and plain-language policies. |

## 5. Product and merchandising model

### Launch assortment principle

Launch a **complete-fit system**, not a youth-fashion department store. Proposed opening range: 10-14 sellable SKUs across one coordinated family, one collectible entry item, and one limited story piece.

### Commercial product roles

| Role | Purpose | Indicative product | Indicative price hypothesis | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core | First purchase and repeat trust | Clean tee, relaxed tee, essential cap | ₹1,299-₹1,899; cap ₹999-₹1,499 | Always available where possible |
| Signature | Attention and AOV | K-line tee, track pant, overshirt, coordinated set | ₹2,199-₹5,499 | Seasonal colors; controlled restock |
| Culture | Meaning and cultural equity | Documented creator capsule | ₹3,999-₹7,999 | Finite, numbered only if operationally true |

Prices are hypotheses, not commitments. Every increase must be justified by visible fabric, technique, construction, scarcity, coordination, or cultural value.

### Hero franchise requirement

The opening franchise is a coordinated **K-Line Set**: a repeatable line/panel construction derived from the CHAKSU K, visible from distance and adaptable across tee, overshirt, track pant, and accessories.

The franchise must have:

- One permanent color: near-black/bone.
- One seasonal color.
- One creator edition.
- A consistent line/panel code.
- Climate and movement testing.
- One-click complete-the-fit purchase.
- Photography for front, back, detail, movement, and three styling modes.

No religious, sacred, or culturally sensitive mark should be used as a decorative shortcut. Cultural references require documented authorship and review.

## 7. Functional requirements

### FR-01: Global header and editorial mega-menu

- Transparent over the hero; switches to an opaque high-contrast state after the hero threshold.
- Keyboard-operable desktop mega-menu and focus-trapped mobile drawer.
- Shop menu shows categories on one side and no more than two editorial/product images on the other.
- Header remains legible on every hero frame through adaptive scrim or state change.
- No navigation label below 12 px; mobile touch targets at least 44×44 px.

**Acceptance:** A keyboard-only user can open, traverse, and close every menu without focus loss. Menu interaction does not shift page layout.

### FR-02: Cinematic hero

- Full viewport on desktop; 78-90svh on mobile to preserve an onward cue.
- One current release, one sentence, one primary CTA, one secondary text link.
- Supports a responsive still or short optimized loop; never loads the supplied 163 MB reference video.
- Clear pause control for moving media.
- Poster image is always available; reduced-motion and data-saving users receive the still.

**Acceptance:** The brand/release is understandable in five seconds with audio off. CTA is visible at 320 px width and 200% zoom.

### FR-03: Collection discovery

- Edited collection grid with 2 columns mobile, 3 tablet, 4 desktop; selected editorial cards may span two cells.
- Sticky filter/sort controls after the collection header.
- Filters: category, size, color, fit, climate/use, availability, and price.
- Product cards show primary image, alternate image on intentional hover, title, price, color count, status, and wishlist.
- No hidden quick-add for size-dependent products; quick-add opens an accessible size selector.
- Empty and zero-result states provide clear recovery.

**Acceptance:** Applying a filter updates URL state, is shareable, survives back navigation, and announces the result count to assistive technology.

### FR-04: Search

- Predictive search across products, collections, and stories.
- Typo tolerance and synonym map for common product terms.
- Recent searches remain local to the browser unless the user consents to account history.
- No-results state recommends categories and contact/fit help.

### FR-05: Product detail page

Desktop uses an image-led left rail and sticky purchase panel; mobile keeps purchase actions close to the selected media and makes the add-to-bag action sticky only after the primary CTA leaves view.

Mandatory PDP proof stack:

1. Product title, price, tax/shipping disclosure.
2. Color and size selection with unavailable/restock states.
3. Model measurements and worn size.
4. Garment measurements and visual fit comparison.
5. Fabric composition, weight/GSM where valid, hand feel, and climate/use context.
6. Construction and technique: stitching, print, wash, embroidery, origin.
7. Movement/detail media.
8. Delivery estimate based on pincode.
9. Payment availability subject to configured provider: prepaid, COD, UPI, cards, or approved installments.
10. Returns/exchange summary with a link to the exact policy.
11. Reviews, customer fits, and fit feedback.
12. Complete-the-fit recommendations.

**Acceptance:** Product, price, selected variant, delivery, and return terms remain consistent from PDP through checkout. No generic copy may be reused if garment facts differ.

### FR-06: Fit concierge

- WhatsApp or onsite help entry appears on PDP and size guide, not as a permanently obstructive floating bubble.
- Starts with product, selected size, height, usual size, and preferred fit when the customer opts in.
- Operating hours and expected response time are truthful.
- Advice is stored only with consent and follows the privacy policy.

### FR-07: Cart and complete-the-fit

- Cart drawer for quick review; full cart route for recovery and accessibility.
- Show variant image, size, color, quantity, delivery estimate, and remove/edit controls.
- Cross-sell no more than two contextually compatible items.
- Bundle benefit must be explicit and must not use a misleading pre-discount anchor.
- Persistent express checkout is allowed only when it does not obscure cart review.

### FR-08: Checkout and payment handoff

- Guest checkout is the default.
- Preserve cart, discounts, locale, attribution, and analytics IDs across handoff.
- Surface COD/UPI/card availability only after merchant/provider validation.
- Never collect raw card data in the CHAKSU application.
- Support GST invoice details if business rules require them.

### FR-09: Account and post-purchase

- Email/phone sign-in using platform-supported secure authentication.
- Orders, tracking, return/exchange initiation, address book, wishlist, and communication preferences.
- Order status language must match operational reality.
- Review request is sent after expected delivery, not immediately after purchase.
- Customer-fit upload includes consent, moderation, and usage-rights language.

### FR-10: Stories and drops

- Editorial story template supports chapters, full-bleed media, pull quotes, credits, product links, and reduced-motion-safe parallax.
- Every story has a commercial handoff without turning the article into a product grid.
- Drop states: tease, waitlist, live, low stock, sold out, restock vote, archive.
- Stock language must be generated from actual inventory thresholds.

### FR-11: Promotions

- Supports codes, bundles, free-shipping thresholds, and customer-specific offers.
- No always-on countdowns or fake urgency.
- Sale is not a primary navigation item at launch.
- Price history and strike-through logic must be lawful, truthful, and platform-consistent.

### FR-12: Content management

Editors can manage hero releases, editorial modules, navigation imagery, stories, size/fit content, policy summaries, and creator credits without deployment.

All commerce-critical facts—inventory, price, variant, tax, and checkout—remain owned by the commerce platform.

## 8. User stories

- As a first-time visitor, I can understand what CHAKSU stands for before being asked to shop.
- As a mobile shopper, I can browse without animation causing lag or losing my position.
- As an uncertain shopper, I can compare my body/garment measurements and ask for fit help.
- As a climate-conscious wearer, I can understand when and how the garment is comfortable to wear.
- As a full-price buyer, I can see the make and service evidence that justifies the price.
- As a returning customer, I can buy a compatible piece without relearning the fit system.
- As an assistive-technology user, I can navigate, filter, select variants, add to cart, and reach checkout.
- As an editor, I can release a drop and its supporting story without developer intervention.
- As operations, I can change policy or delivery summaries once and have them update consistently.
- As product, I can see which objections, sizes, products, and stories influence purchase and return behavior.

## 9. Measurement framework

### North-star metric

**90-day full-price repeat-customer rate**, segmented by first product role and acquisition source.

### Primary launch metrics

| Metric | Direction / initial gate | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero franchise PDP conversion | Above site average after meaningful traffic | Product-message fit |
| Full-price sell-through | Calibrate by inventory; use 30-day cohort | Demand quality |
| Size-related return rate | Down across two cohorts | Fit promise accuracy |
| Complete-fit attach rate | Up without excessive discount | AOV and franchise strength |
| Save/share to qualified-session rate | Above account median | Cultural relevance that reaches commerce |
| Owned-audience opt-in | Weekly growth with source consent | Future launch efficiency |
| Delivery promise accuracy | ≥95% orders within displayed window | Operational trust |
| Support first response | Set only after staffing is confirmed | Service credibility |

Do not hard-code aspirational targets until the current baseline, margin, inventory, and traffic quality are known.

### Event taxonomy

Required events include `view_release`, `hero_cta`, `view_collection`, `apply_filter`, `search`, `view_item`, `view_fit_guide`, `play_fit_video`, `open_proof`, `check_pincode`, `start_fit_help`, `select_variant`, `add_to_cart`, `view_cart`, `add_complete_fit`, `begin_checkout`, `purchase`, `request_return`, `submit_review`, `submit_customer_fit`, and `join_drop_waitlist`.

Every event must include a documented schema, consent category, owner, and data-retention policy.

## 10. Accessibility and inclusive requirements

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA for the complete purchase journey.
- Provide visible focus, correct landmarks, semantic headings, skip links, and form errors tied to fields.
- Minimum body text 16 px; metadata may be 12-14 px with sufficient contrast and non-critical use.
- Touch targets at least 44×44 px on mobile.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; motion is never the only way content is revealed.
- Captions/transcripts for meaningful video and alt text for editorial/product imagery.
- Do not encode availability, validation, or selection using color alone.
- Support 200% zoom, text reflow, keyboard-only checkout handoff, and screen-reader variant selection.
- Tamil or other Indian-language content must use native-script fonts and human-reviewed copy, never image-only text.

## 11. Non-functional requirements

- Core Web Vitals at p75 mobile: LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1.
- Initial route JavaScript budget: ≤180 KB gzip excluding consented third-party code; justify exceptions.
- Hero still ≤400 KB AVIF/WebP at common desktop viewport; mobile derivative ≤220 KB.
- No autoplay asset over 3 MB for the first load; video loads after poster/intent.
- Availability target: 99.9% storefront, excluding hosted checkout incidents.
- Server-side and client-side monitoring with alert ownership.
- Search-engine crawlability for collections, products, stories, policies, and structured data.
- Privacy-by-design consent, retention, deletion, and least-privilege access.
- Compatible with current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari, and the latest two major mobile OS browser generations.

## 12. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Consequence | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Style overwhelms commerce | High bounce and low confidence | Five-second testing; proof and CTA hierarchy are release gates. |
| Parallax causes jank or nausea | Performance/accessibility failure | Transform-only motion, capped amplitudes, reduced-motion fallback, device budgets. |
| Product facts vary by SKU | Trust and returns | Required structured fields; block publication when proof fields are incomplete. |
| False cultural signaling | Credibility damage | Creator credits, contextual review, documented rights and authorship. |
| Large media payload | Slow mobile experience | Responsive derivatives, poster-first video, CDN, lazy loading, strict budgets. |
| Conflicting policy copy | Conversion and support tax | One structured policy source and automated consistency tests. |
| Fake scarcity or discount dependence | Full-price trust erosion | Inventory-driven states; no continuous sale placement. |
| Platform/payment assumptions fail in India | Checkout blockers | Validate merchant eligibility, UPI, COD, tax, and invoicing during sprint zero. |

## 13. Launch gates

The website does not launch until:

1. Positioning and hero franchise are approved.
2. Every live SKU passes the product-proof completeness check.
3. Delivery and return language is identical across PDP, cart, checkout, and help.
4. Payment/COD/UPI and tax behavior is validated with real sandbox orders.
5. Accessibility, performance, security, analytics, SEO, and rollback checks pass.
6. A mobile low-bandwidth test passes using production media.
7. Customer support has operating hours, macros, escalation, and return ownership.
8. Inventory, low-stock, sold-out, waitlist, and restock states are tested end to end.
